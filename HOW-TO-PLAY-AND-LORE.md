# IRON CROWN — The Player's Handbook and Chronicle

A fictional Victorian railway kingdom. For 2–8 tycoons using a television/PC for the shared map and phones, tablets or PCs for private decisions.

## How to Play — 60 seconds

1. **Join:** Scan the TV QR code (or enter the four-letter code) on your phone. Give yourself a name and choose one of eight available tycoons.
2. **Keep your destinations:** Each player privately gets three Destination Tickets. Keep at least two; nobody else needs to see them.
3. **Found two Depots:** The game uses snake-order placement. Wait for your turn, then choose a vacant production tile.
4. **Earn Income:** Your land and railway lines pay resources/Gold automatically every round.
5. **Plan two secret orders:** On your controller choose two legal actions, in either order, then press Lock In. Research can only be taken once per round.
6. **Watch the TV reveal:** Each player's orders resolve and clashes are shown. Your tickets remain hidden on your controller.
7. **Claim the Crown:** First to 10 Prestige in Quick, or 15 in Standard/Advanced at a Reveal, wins. Otherwise the most Prestige at the round limit wins. Nobody gets eliminated.

Every round is **Income → Plan → Reveal**. The kingdom progresses through **Steam → Iron → Electric**. You receive one free Upgrade at each new era.

### The seven actions

| Action | Cost | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build Depot | 1 Timber + 1 Grain | Claim a free adjacent tile. |
| Upgrade City | 2 Grain + 2 Steel + 1 Coal | Upgrade a Market Town you own. |
| Lay Track | 1 Timber + 1 Steel per route segment | Claim a full marked route, connected to your holding. |
| Recruit | 2 Gold per Soldier, maximum 3 | Reinforce a tile you own. |
| Trade | No action cost | Exchange with the bank (normally 3:1) or send a timed offer. |
| Research | 3 Gold + 1 Coal | Draw a new secret ticket or buy an available current-era Upgrade. Once per round. |
| Attack | Commit Soldiers | Attack an adjacent enemy tile; roll one die plus troops and bonuses. Defender wins ties. |

Scoring: Market Town Depot **+1**, City **+2**, completed Destination Ticket **+2–4**, Longest Railway **+2**, Largest Army **+2**, Grand Station **+2**. In Advanced mode, each unfinished ticket deducts one Prestige.

## How to host on TV, computer or tablet

Open your game's main address on the shared screen. Leave that browser tab open to show the QR, board, cards, Gazette, player standings and sound controls.

Each person opens /phone.html on their phone or another computer browser to keep their own actions and contracts private. On a single PC use **two browser windows**: one shared host display and one player/controller display. Avoid opening the same human seat in multiple windows; rejoin from its original controller.

**Sound:** On the TV choose "♫ Sound off" to enable the recorded effects and music, then "⚙ Audio" to choose Quiet, Balanced or Cinematic. The controller has its own "♪ Off" button for optional short selection, confirmation and dice cues. Sound is opt-in on each device; use Balanced first for conversation. The pack contains 17 locally served recordings (16 generated with Mirelo, one original Sonilo instrumental).

### Finding the help

The TV/PC top bar has **? How to Play** and **History & Lore**. The TV lobby also has **Learn to Play — 60 seconds** and **Discover the Kingdom**. The controller always has **? How to Play**, **Story** and its sound control, plus a quick-start link on the Join page. Help is available during a game, not only before a game starts.

## THE CHRONICLE OF IRON CROWN — FICTION

**The following kingdom, succession and stories are fictional.** This is alternate-history flavour for the game, not a claim about a real monarchy.

### The last proclamation

The old king's train never arrived. Within days, the royal ministers announced a desperate compromise. The crown would go not to the loudest noble, but to the builder who could bind the divided nation together. Whoever brought goods from the coalfields, grain from farms, steel from mills and people between the great towns would carry the Iron Crown.

The proclamation sounded noble. The bidders heard only opportunity. Some regarded railways as modern miracles; others saw ancient estates, livelihoods and entire cities that could be won or lost. The Crown's railway charters triggered a contest that would outlive the old court.

### Steam — the first wagers

Smoke spills over small stations, maps are spread on polished oak tables and surveyors tramp across cold fields. Timber and Grain build the first Depots. The mines and farms keep the engine boilers fed. The rival tycoons meet in railway hotels, and not all of their agreements are written down.

### Iron — the kingdom remade

Factories grow taller. Steel girders replace old crossings. The engineers demand bridges; the banks demand profits; workers demand fairness. Faster engines push beyond the first towns. The question is no longer who owns a line, but who will control an entire network.

### Electric — the final reckoning

Telegraph wires and electric lights announce a new century. Station halls shine, contracts arrive by wire and the rival empires become hard to untangle. The ancient throne stands empty. A final score of Prestige will name the builder of the kingdom.

### The eight claimants

- **Lady Augusta Blackwood — the Duchess:** Sees a railway crown as an obligation to preserve the family estate, even if she must embrace radical industry.
- **Silas Brunel-Crane — the Engineer:** Wants proof that a skilled designer with a drawing board can rival any inherited title.
- **"Lucky" Jack Hartigan — the Gambler:** Measures a risky junction the way others assess a hand of cards.
- **Mrs Ada Pennyworth — the Banker:** Believes no kingdom can be governed without its ledgers balancing.
- **Colonel Rupert Fairfax — the Colonel:** Defends order and fears a railway war fought as much in boardrooms as on hillsides.
- **Nell Thackery — the Union Boss:** Insists that the people who swing the hammers must be remembered when the gold is counted.
- **Professor Ottoline Vex — the Inventor:** Sees the Electric age as an invitation to redesign the entire kingdom.
- **Mr Ezra Coldwell — the Smuggler:** Understands the quiet paths between official depots, ports and warehouses.

These biographies are narrative colour. The established gameplay perks remain Advanced-only, as in the original game specification.

## A SHORT HISTORY OF THE REAL RAILWAY AGE

This section contains real events that inspired the tone of the three fictional eras. The game deliberately condenses decades into twelve (or eight) rounds.

| Year | Historical milestone | Why it inspired Iron Crown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **15 September 1830** | The Liverpool and Manchester Railway opened; a landmark in steam-powered intercity passenger and goods transport. | Steam-age locomotives, new rail investments and route-building. |
| **1856** | Henry Bessemer patented his process for producing steel more cheaply. | The Iron era's cities, steelworks, bridges and stronger infrastructure. |
| **1880s** | Railways already used telegraph signalling, and electric lighting appeared in some stations and public buildings. | The Electric era's communication, illuminated stations and final technological race. |

Historical references:
- Science Museum Group — Liverpool and Manchester Railway: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp29532/liverpool-and-manchester-railway
- Science Museum Group — Sample of Bessemer Steel: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co19965/sample-of-bessemer-steel
- London Museum — When did London get electricity?: https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/when-did-london-get-electricity/

Electricity did **not** become commonplace in most British homes in the 1880s; adoption came later. The Iron Crown setting is an invented industrial country spanning the 1830s–1900s, not a literal chronology of Britain.

## Accessibility and device advice

The TV/PC shared board scales to very large and narrow screens; the private controller reflows for phones, tablets and PC windows. Text generally starts at 16 px on phones; targets remain large enough for touch. Keyboard users can activate rulebook chapters and highlighted legal map targets; dialogues support Escape. Both TV and controller observe Reduced Motion.

**Remaining real-world acceptance:** actual 320 px phones and multiple brands, TV overscan and speaker balance, iPhone/Android reconnect behaviour, full WCAG AA measurements across every scene and uninterrupted production storage across Render restarts. Automated checks do not prove these are finished.
